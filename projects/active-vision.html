Active Vision
on Reduced Hardware

Question

Video segmentation models usually spend the same compute on every pixel of every frame. Human vision does not: a sharp fovea moves across a blurry periphery. This project asks whether a segmentation model on a single T4-class GPU (16 GB) can do the same, look at less of each frame, and still match the full frame at equal latency. It uses public data only.

I do not build a new segmentation model. I add a layer on top of existing real-time backbones, PIDNet-S and BiSeNetV2: preprocessing (a low-resolution context view plus high-resolution crops), an optional persistent scene memory (a canvas), and a gaze policy that chooses which regions to inspect.

Protocol

  • Data: Cityscapes-VPS. Tuning on VPS train (400 clips, 2,400 labelled frames), reporting once on VPS val (50 clips, 300 labelled frames), on finalists frozen beforehand.
  • Metric: 19-class mIoU, with 95% clip bootstrap intervals paired against the full frame on the same clips.
  • Latency: the judge is a Tesla T4 at batch 1, FP16, with CUDA graphs. Eager mode is launch-bound, so graphs are required for any comparison to mean something.
  • Rule: a foveated variant wins only if it lands above the full-frame mIoU–latency curve. Pass rules are written down before the runs.

Methods and results

Method 1Full frame
Reference.The data layer, ported backbones and evaluation harness reproduce the published Cityscapes val numbers (PIDNet-S 78.76 vs 78.8, BiSeNetV2 74.95 vs 74.95).
Method 2Glimpses, no memory
Negative.46 of 55 finalists fall below the full-frame curve on val, and the other 9 are dominated. The best (BiSeNetV2, 0.5 context + 4 fused 384 px glimpses) is −0.5 mIoU at equal latency, about 7.7 ms slower at equal mIoU.
Method 3Motion-gated reuse
Negative on train.None of 1,296 configurations per model beats the curve. Deciding what to recompute costs about 6.8 ms, a frame-old prediction loses about 5 points, and motion is a poor proxy for error.
Method 4Persistent canvas
Stopped at the bound.With a greedy oracle selector that costs nothing, none of 693 configurations reaches the curve. Best: −0.85 mIoU at equal latency against a +1.0 pass rule, so the learned policy was not built.

What I take from it

The glimpse is the ceiling. A frozen model on crops stays below the same model on the whole frame at scale 0.75, whatever is selected or remembered, and on a T4 that slightly larger full frame is cheap. Negative results with a fixed protocol are still results: each one rules out a family of designs before anyone spends a month training a policy for it.
